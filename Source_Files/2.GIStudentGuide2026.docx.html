Student Guide 2026

Course description

Learning gastrointestinal system; approach to common clinical presentations, structures and functions, pathogenesis, pathophysiology, and pathological changes of diseases and disorders in gastrointestinal system which are of global health importance.

Course policies

The GI module course policy is similar to previous modules, but please review them as below. Recent updates or items related to recent incidents will be highlighted in yellow.

     Attendance policy

  • Mandatory Sessions (🙋): 100% attendance required.
  • Tardiness: More than 15 minutes late = absent.
  • Dress Code & Conduct: Follow Student Life rules (reviewed on First Day).

Absences

  • Absences = “Poor” score for in-class evaluation.
  • Planned Absence: Contact the module coordinator as early as possible to discuss Zoom attendance or make-up class.
  • Process:
  1. Submit absence form to course staff (forwarded to module coordinator).
  2. Complete assigned make-up work (see GI remedial work specification).
  3. Submit remedial work to cu-medi-gi@chula.md by  Friday 5:00 PM of the same week (unless specified otherwise).
  4. The instructor reviews the reason and work quality.
  5. If unsatisfactory → escalated to the Course Committee → Professionalism Committee.

Excused absences include:

  1. Sick Leave
  • If a student takes sick leave, they are required to provide a reason for the sick leave.
  • Submission of a medical certificate from an approved hospital may be required in some situations. Please contact the module coordinators for more details.

2. Personal Business Leave

  • Personal Business Leave is meant to cover situations where students need to take time off to attend government-related activities, calls to active military duty or jury duty, religious holy days, official University activities, and where these requirements can't be satisfied on weekends or public holidays. Other excused absences include documented family members' illnesses, family deaths, and other documented crises.
  • The students must submit the form 3 working days before the personal business leave, except in emergency situations.

Both sick and personal leave forms must be signed and sent to the module coordinator. Link for leave form 

Reminder

Attendance will be recorded at the actual class location. For any sessions held outside Smart Classroom 1, attendance will be checked at the activity site based on the time you arrive and join. Fingerprint scans at the entrance of Smart Classroom 1 will not be counted.

Falsifying attendance (e.g., signing for someone else, scanning in then leaving) is a serious professionalism violation and illegal. Such incidents will be recorded in the professionalism record and may lead to an Unsatisfactory (U) grade.
        Failure to meet attendance, complete remedial work, or uphold professionalism standards may result in a grade of Unsatisfactory (U).

Non-Mandatory Sessions

        Students may attend in person or view recorded content.

  • If attending in person, punctuality and respectful behavior are expected to avoid disrupting peers.

In clinic attendance policy

  • As members of the Faculty of Medicine, Chulalongkorn University, maintaining high ethical standards and integrity are absolutely crucial.
  • Consider your actions very carefully.
  • Medical students have freedom of speech, but speech must be respectful. All discussion or social talk with friends in the elevator should be avoided.
  • Video recording and photography are not allowed in all clinical care areas.
  • Always bring your white coat and name badge if you will be in a clinical setting. Your coat must be clean, pressed,  fully buttoned, and worn at all times during every patient encounter either real or simulated.

In person dress code

  • Clothing should not have rips, tears, or frayed edges. Clothing should allow for an appropriate range of movement, and should not be  flashy or draw attention. Clothing should not be revealing, and do not expose your midriff.
  • Button-down shirts, professional tops, or blouses should be worn. A low-cut or plunging neckline is inappropriate.
  • Tank tops, T-shirts, and thin or “spaghetti-style” straps are not allowed.
  • Skirts or dresses are appropriate. Legs should be covered to the knee.
  • Do not wear jeans, cropped pants or shorts.
  • For gentlemen, a tie is recommended during patient encounters but not mandatory. Crew-length socks are appropriate. Do not expose your ankle.
  • Dress shoes, low heels, or flats should be worn. Flip-flops, colorful sneakers, and crocs are not appropriate.
  • Hospital scrubs are not considered professional attire while on campus. They are reserved for operating rooms and emergency room environments.
  • Wear your name tag in an easily viewable location (collar or chest pocket of coat, top, or dress).
  • Tattoos or other forms of body art must be covered.
  • Jewelry should be minimal and understated. Tongue rings and ear gauges are not allowed.
  • Fingernails should be trimmed, and clean. Flamboyant decoration or painting is  improper.
  • Hair should be cleaned and neat. It should not interfere with your activity and should not touch patients during the examination. Bright and fancy hair coloring should be avoided. Facial hair should be neatly trimmed.

AI Usage Policy in this Module

  • For Learning Purposes: Be cautious—information generated by AI may be inaccurate. AI-generated answers should not be used as references.
  • Working: Developing proficiency in systematic thinking and documentation of medical notes is a key learning outcome. Do not rely on AI as your primary resource. If you choose to use AI, limit it to language validation, and always ensure you fully understand the content to gain the most from your learning.

Course assessment (S/U)

In-class evaluation

During clinical skills classes, clinic visits, group discussions, team-based learning, and other designated sessions, instructors and facilitators will evaluate students across multiple domains, including but not limited to critical thinking, professionalism, digital literacy, academic presentation, communication of scientific concepts, leadership qualities, and teamwork skills.

Performance Scale:        4 = Excellent

3 = Average

2 = Below Average

1 = Poor (including absent)

        Criteria for Satisfactory (S)

Students will receive a grade of Satisfactory (S) if they meet all of the following conditions:

  1. Attendance: 100% participation in all mandatory activities with attendance checks.
  2. Behavior:
  1. Satisfactory performance as evaluated by facilitators.
  2. Successful completion of assigned remediation for any absence from a mandatory class.
  3. No serious professionalism violations (e.g., falsifying attendance).

Criteria for Unsatisfactory (U)
         A grade of Unsatisfactory (U) will be assigned, and the incident recorded in the Professionalism Report, under any of the following conditions:

  1. Attendance: Less than 100% participation in mandatory activities without successful remediation.
  1. Submission of make-up work that is of poor quality, lacks independent critical thinking, or contains significant errors.
  2. Failure to submit required remediation by the stated deadline (Friday, 5:00 PM of the same week) without appropriate reason.
  1. Behavior:
  1. In-Class Performance: Receipt of “Below Average (2)” in three or more in-class assessments, or “Poor (1)” in two or more in-class assessments.
  2. Serious professionalism violations including unexcused absent from formative assessment.
